# Underwriting and risk selection

Revenue in insurance is easy to grow badly: cut the price or loosen the terms on a risk every other
carrier declined, book the premium today, and pay for it in the loss ratio for the next three years.
Underwriting is the discipline of matching coverage terms and price to the actual hazard before the
carrier is bound.

## Submission triage and risk appetite

Not every complete submission deserves a quote. Screen against appetite and eligibility gates first
so underwriting hours go to risks the carrier actually wants to write:

- **Appetite and class eligibility.** Check the primary and secondary operations (NAICS/SIC, ISO
  class code, or group industry classification), geographic footprint, catastrophe zone aggregation,
  and prohibited hazard list. A risk with 15% of its payroll or revenue in an excluded operation is
  that operation on the day a severe loss occurs.
- **Submission completeness.** Require a signed application, three to five years of currently valued
  company loss runs (valued within 60 to 90 days of effective date), exposure schedules (statement
  of values, vehicle schedule, payroll by class, or census with age/tier/zip and prior carrier
  experience), and financial or safety controls before releasing a bindable quote. Quoting subject
  to loss runs invites adverse selection.

## Loss run, frequency, and severity analysis

Past losses do not repeat identically, but the structure of a loss history tells you whether the
risk has a control problem or had a single bad afternoon.

- **Separate frequency from severity.** High frequency of small claims (slip-and-falls, minor cargo
  damage, routine ergonomic strains, or high high-utilization dental/medical claims) predicts future
  losses reliably and points to management, maintenance, or plan-design gaps. A single shock loss
  requires investigating whether the underlying hazard still exists and whether excess layers are
  priced for the tail.
- **Develop immature years and adjust for exposure growth.** Losses from the current policy year are
  incomplete because incurred-but-not-reported (IBNR) claims and open case reserves are still
  developing. Normalize historical losses against historical exposure units (payroll, revenue,
  square footage, power units, or covered lives) so a fast-growing insured's older years are not
  understated.
- **Inspect open reserves and large-loss narratives.** On any claim exceeding threshold, read the
  loss description, cause of loss, paid-to-incurred ratio, and corrective action taken by the
  insured.

## Pricing, schedule rating, and terms structure

Price and terms are two halves of the same equation. A risk that is uninsurable at a $1,000
deductible on an unendorsed base form may be profitable with a higher deductible, a sub-limit, or a
targeted exclusion.

- **Start from the technical (actuarial) rate.** Apply manual base rates, territory and class
  relativities, increased limits factors (ILFs), deductible credits, and experience modification
  factors (E-Mod or credibility-weighted group experience) before applying underwriter judgment.
- **Support every schedule debit or credit with documented risk characteristics.** State regulators
  audit schedule rating files during market conduct examinations (`legal-risk:regulatory-compliance`).
  Every credit or debit for premises/operations, safety programs, management cooperation, or
  workforce stability must cite objective evidence in the file — never use schedule rating as an
  undocumented plug to hit a broker's target price.
- **Structure endorsements and subjectivities explicitly.** List every mandatory and optional
  endorsement, sub-limit, coinsurance clause, waiting period, or protective-safeguard warranty on
  the quote specimen. Distinguish **pre-bind subjectivities** (which must be satisfied before
  coverage attaches) from **post-bind subjectivities** (which carry a strict deadline and
  cancellation trigger if not delivered).

## Binding authority and treaty boundaries

An underwriter's letter of authority is a hard boundary backed by the carrier's reinsurance treaties
and state filings.

- **Check capacity, aggregation, and reinsurance exclusions.** Confirm total insured value (TIV),
  policy limits, attachment points, and catastrophe PML/AAL aggregation stay inside your individual
  authority and treaty automatic capacity. Risks exceeding authority or requiring facultative
  reinsurance placement require written referral approval before a bindable indication or quote is
  issued.
- **Leave no ambiguity on whether coverage is bound.** Clearly label indications as non-bindable,
  state a quote expiration date, and never issue a binder or policy number until all pre-bind
  subjectivities are cleared and written bind instructions are received.

## Never

- Issue a bindable quote or bind coverage above your written underwriting authority or outside reinsurance treaty parameters without documented referral approval.
- Apply schedule rating credits or debits as an unsupported pricing plug to match a competitor's quote without documenting the risk characteristics required by the state filing.
- Bind coverage with unresolved pre-bind subjectivities or missing currently valued loss runs without an explicit written exception from underwriting leadership.
- Ignore historical exposure changes when trending multi-year loss runs across a growing or shrinking account.
- Leave an ambiguous email or verbal assurance to a broker or agent that could be construed as an oral binder of coverage.
