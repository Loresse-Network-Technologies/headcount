## Insurance carrier, TPA, and payer regulatory obligations

Insurance is regulated state by state in the United States, layered underneath federal mandates for
health, privacy, and financial sanctions. An obligation map for a carrier, managing general agent
(MGA), or third-party administrator (TPA) must track five distinct regimes that run on strict
statutory clocks:

- **State Unfair Claims Settlement Practices and prompt-pay laws.** Every jurisdiction sets binding
  windows to acknowledge a claim, request additional information, accept or deny coverage after proof
  of loss, and issue payment, along with statutory interest penalties when a clean claim is paid
  late. Market conduct examinations sample closed and denied claim files directly against these
  clocks — see `insurance-claims:claims-supervision-and-qa`.
- **ERISA, ACA, No Surprises Act, and mental health parity (for health and dental payers).**
  Employee benefit plans carry federal requirements for Summary Plan Descriptions, adverse benefit
  determination notices, internal and external appeal rights (`insurance-claims:policyholder-and-provider-appeals`),
  out-of-network balance-billing protections, and Non-Quantitative Treatment Limitation (NQTL)
  comparative analyses.
- **Producer, adjuster, and TPA licensing.** Individuals adjudicating claims or soliciting risk must
  hold active resident and non-resident licenses in the required states, and TPAs must maintain
  certificates of authority, surety bonds, and annual state filings where they administer benefits
  or claims.
- **OFAC sanctions and anti-money laundering (AML).** Screening policyholders, claimants,
  beneficiaries, providers, and payees against the OFAC SDN list is a strict-liability obligation.
  Confirmed matches require blocking or rejecting the transaction and reporting to OFAC within 10
  business days — see `insurance-claims:siu-and-sanctions-investigation`.
- **Rate, form, and market conduct record retention.** Policy forms, endorsements, and rating
  manuals must match state SERFF filings where required, and claim files, underwriting files,
  complaint logs, and declination records must be retained intact for the full statutory examination
  window.
